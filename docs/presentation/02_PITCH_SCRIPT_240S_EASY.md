# 02. Pitch Script: 240 Seconds of Glory (Ultra-Easy English)

> **Format:** 4 Minutes Total (Target run-time: **3 minutes 50 seconds** so you finish safely before the 4:00 buzzer).  
> **Style:** Short, simple sentences. Easy words to pronounce. Zero tongue-twisters.  
> **Tip:** Speak slowly. Smile. Pause for one second between sections.

---

## The 4 Timing Blocks at a Glance

```
0:00 ────[ 45 sec: WHO ]────> 0:45 ────[ 60 sec: WHY ]────> 1:45 ────[ 60 sec: WHAT/DEMO ]────> 2:45 ────[ 70 sec: SO WHAT ]────> 3:55 (STOP)
```

---

## Block 1: WHO — Attention & Authenticity (0:00 – 0:45)
**Time:** 45 seconds | **Target words:** ~90 words  
*Goal: Grab the judges' attention in the first 15 seconds.*

### What to show on screen:
- **0:00 – 0:15:** Presenter on camera (or title slide) looking confident and happy.
- **0:15 – 0:45:** Show a cool 3D view of the Moon's South Pole with dark craters and tall peaks.

### Spoken Script:
> *(Smile, look directly at judges/camera)*
>
> "Imagine landing a five-hundred-million-dollar robot on the Moon. 
> 
> But within twenty minutes, its solar panels go completely dark. The temperature drops to minus two hundred degrees. The battery freezes, and all radio signal back to Earth dies. The mission is lost.
>
> That is not a movie. That is the real danger facing NASA’s Artemis missions at the Moon’s South Pole today.
>
> We are **Team LunaMission** [or say your team name]. We are passionate students and developers, and we built the navigation brain that solves this problem."

---

## Block 2: WHY — Create Empathy for the Problem (0:45 – 1:45)
**Time:** 60 seconds | **Target words:** ~125 words  
*Goal: Explain the danger so simply that anyone nods along.*

### What to show on screen:
- **0:45 – 1:15:** A slide or diagram showing the Sun low on the horizon and a mountain casting a huge shadow.
- **1:15 – 1:45:** Graphic showing Earth wobbling near the horizon behind a crater wall.

### Spoken Script:
> "Why is the Moon’s South Pole so tricky?
>
> Because the Moon barely tilts. The Sun never rises high in the sky. It stays very low near the ground, like an endless sunset. 
>
> If a mountain fifty kilometers away stands in the way, it casts a shadow over one hundred kilometers long!
>
> At the same time, the Earth slowly wobbles in the sky. A crater rim can easily block your radio signal back home.
>
> And here is the worst part: **Launch Delays**. 
> 
> If bad weather delays the rocket launch by just four days, the Moon rotates. All the shadows move! A sunny spot can turn into pitch-black night.
>
> Today, mission planners have to calculate this across messy spreadsheets. We asked: why can’t they see it in seconds?"

---

## Block 3: WHAT — The Big Idea & Live Demo (1:45 – 2:45)
**Time:** 60 seconds | **Target words:** ~130 words  
*Goal: Show the working web app! This is where you win the hackathon.*

### What to do on screen:
1. **At 1:45:** Show **Site Map** (`Alt+1`). Click on **Connecting Ridge (CR01)** near Shackleton Crater.
2. **At 2:05:** Press **`Alt + 2`** to jump to the **Analyze** screen. Point mouse at the brown mountain line, yellow Sun dot, and blue Earth dot.
3. **At 2:30:** Point mouse at the timeline at the bottom, then drag the **Launch-Slip slider** to the right (+3 days).

### Spoken Script:
> *(Screen switches to web app)*
>
> "This is **LunaMission**.
>
> *(Click Connecting Ridge on Map)*  
> We start here on our South Polar Map. We use real NASA laser satellite scans of the lunar surface. Let’s pick **Connecting Ridge**, right next to Shackleton Crater.
>
> *(Press Alt+2 to switch to Analyze)*  
> Instantly, our app draws what we call the **360-Degree Sky-Ribbon**. 
>
> This brown line is the real mountain skyline around our lander. The yellow dot is the Sun, and the blue dot is Earth. 
> 
> Look right here: even when the Sun is supposed to be up, this mountain ridge blocks the light and creates a shadow!
>
> Down below, our timeline turns yellow when you have solar power, and blue when you can talk to Earth.
>
> *(Drag the Launch-Slip slider)*  
> And if the rocket is delayed by three days? We drag this slider, and the entire mission timeline recalculates in one second!"

---

## Block 4: SO WHAT NEXT — Impact & The Finish (2:45 – 3:55)
**Time:** 70 seconds | **Target words:** ~145 words  
*Goal: Show the Compare screen, quick peek at 3D Rover, say the big impact, and stop cleanly.*

### What to do on screen:
1. **At 2:45:** Press **`Alt + 3`** to switch to the **Compare** screen. Show two landing sites side-by-side.
2. **At 3:15:** Press **`Alt + 4`** to switch to the **3D Lab**. Drive the rover forward with `W`, press `H` to toggle the helmet visor HUD.
3. **At 3:35:** Cut back to presenter or title screen for the final powerful closing sentence.

### Spoken Script:
> *(Press Alt+3 for Compare)*  
> "Planners need to pick the best site out of many. 
> 
> In our **Compare Workspace**, we put candidate spots through a tournament filter. It eliminates sites with steep slopes or too much darkness, showing engineers the safest winning spot.
>
> *(Press Alt+4 for 3D Lab, drive rover for 3 seconds, press H for HUD)*  
> And for astronauts on the surface, our **3D Lab** lets crews test rover routes and look at mountain horizons through an astronaut helmet visor.
>
> *(Camera on presenter or final title card)*  
> LunaMission is fully tested with over sixteen hundred automated checks and real NASA data. 
>
> It turns weeks of difficult rocket science into a fast, visual flight deck for scientists, astronauts, and students.
>
> We are LunaMission. Helping humanity take its safest steps on the Moon. Thank you!"
>
> *(Stop speaking at 3:55. Smile. Wait for applause!)*

---

## 👥 How to Split Between 2 or 3 Speakers (Optional)

If your team wants to share speaking:
- **Speaker 1 (The Hook & Problem):** Reads Block 1 and Block 2 (`0:00 - 1:45`).
- **Speaker 2 (The Live Demo):** Reads Block 3 (`1:45 - 2:45`) while clicking the app.
- **Speaker 3 (The Impact & Closer):** Reads Block 4 (`2:45 - 3:55`) and delivers the final "Thank you".
